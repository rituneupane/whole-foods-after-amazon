# Notes

Three pages. One story each.

## Overview

The 10-K line everyone quotes is Physical stores. Whole Foods is nearly all of it. Amazon Fresh and Go were small and are gone. So this dashboard treats that series as Whole Foods store sales.

The 2020 dip is real: dollars moved from the store into Online stores (pickup and delivery). A scorecard that only watches the 10-K line that year would say the banner shrank. The store did not stop working — the trip changed.

Fresh is the largest department because meat, seafood, dairy, and bakery sit together. Produce is the identity category. Prepared is the only group still taking share. California is about one in five dollars. New York and Massachusetts sell more per store than the store count implies, so density is the question, not new doors.

## 365

365 is Whole Foods’ Kirkland. It does the price work so the rest of the store can stay Whole Foods.

It is 24.8% of grocery and 11.6% of all sales because it lives in center store (oats, beans, water, oil), not in produce. Growing mix from here is a SKU and placement job, not a new brand job.

The 12-item basket is the argument against “Whole Paycheck.” $57 vs $77 for national brands on the same shelf, $58 at Kroger, $48 at Trader Joe’s. Sit next to Kroger. Do not chase TJ’s.

## Prime

Prime members went from about a third of store dollars (2018) to just over half (2026 YTD). That is Amazon’s customer file inside Whole Foods.

Pickup and delivery are 20% of the store. Amazon books those trips in Online stores, so they never show up in the Physical stores line. If you only manage the 10-K number, you are steering the Prime trip with the lights off.

17 million households already shop both Amazon and Whole Foods. A 365 pantry bundle on Prime delivery rides that file without a new store.
